What I do

I take on focused pieces of AI and cyber security work, from early research and methodology through to hands on testing and clear reporting for senior audiences.

AI security research and methodology

Scoping messy, open ended problems, mapping the technology and market landscape, and building repeatable ways to decide where effort should go. This is the kind of work I led for LASR, including its Opportunity Book and market analysis.

AI supply chain security

Working through provenance, dependencies and the trust gaps in AI enabled systems, and helping teams move from simple visibility towards controls they can rely on. This is the subject of my co authored paper with Cisco.

Agentic AI security

Looking at how more autonomous AI systems change the attack surface, and what that means in practice for organisations adopting them. I have briefed researchers and policymakers on this and had strong feedback from senior stakeholders.

Testing and evaluation

Designing test frameworks, standing up environments and synthetic datasets, and running structured evaluations so that decisions rest on evidence rather than vendor claims.

Translating risk for decision makers

Turning deep technical detail into briefings and reports that government and industry stakeholders can act on. Much of my work has been presented to senior audiences and used to inform strategy and investment.

A bit about me

My foundation is in applied mathematics and I still think like a mathematician. I like finding the real world anchor for an abstract problem, which is what pulled me from modelling volcanoes towards securing AI in the first place. The most useful thing I bring is that intersection, deep technical work paired with the ability to explain it plainly to the people who have to decide what to do.

Outside work I compete and coach in Brazilian jiu jitsu, and I have taught women only classes to help keep a safe space for women to train. I founded Ethical Guildford, a community group for conversations about equality and the environment, and I have volunteered with Sue Ryder since school. Giving back matters to me and it tends to shape the work I take on.
